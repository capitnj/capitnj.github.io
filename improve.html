Improve your chances

What actually moves the needle

General guidance — not specific to any one school, but useful for almost every application.

Academics & testing
  • Colleges weigh trend more than any single grade — an upward GPA trajectory junior→senior year reads well even if freshman year was rough.
  • If your GPA is below a school's average, look into applying test-optional, or submit a strong score if it's above their average.
  • Retaking the SAT/ACT once, with real prep in between, is usually worth it if your first score was a practice-level attempt.
  • Talk to your counselor about which AP/Honors courses actually strengthen your application vs. which just add stress.
Essays & applications
  • Specific beats impressive. A small, honest story about one real moment usually reads better than a resume of achievements.
  • Write your first draft without worrying about the word count — cut it down after, don't self-censor while writing.
  • Have one teacher and one non-teacher (parent, mentor, friend) read your essay — you want both perspectives.
  • Answer supplemental "why us" essays with details specific to that school — generic answers are easy for admissions to spot.
Financial aid & scholarships
  • File the FAFSA as early as possible after it opens — some aid is first-come, first-served.
  • Private colleges often require the CSS Profile in addition to the FAFSA — check each school's requirements early.
  • Local scholarships (your school, town, a parent's employer) have far less competition than national ones — apply to those first.
  • Appeal a financial aid offer if your situation changes or another school offered more — many schools will reconsider.
Extracurriculars & fit
  • Depth beats breadth — colleges would rather see two years of real commitment than one line on ten different clubs.
  • Leadership doesn't have to mean "president of something" — starting a small initiative or running one, even informally, counts.
  • If you work a part-time job or have family responsibilities, say so — colleges read that as real-world time management, not a gap.
  • Apply to a genuine mix of reach, match, and safety schools — CapItNJ's fit score is a starting point, not a guarantee.
This is general guidance, not personalized advice for your specific situation — for anything about your individual chances, talk to your school counselor.
